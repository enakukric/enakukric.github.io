Google Ads: Protecting & rebuilding search demand during a brand name change

My role · Performance Marketer (Paid Acquisition)

Marketing StrategyProject ManagementCross-Functional Team ManagementPartnership ManagementPaid Social ManagementGraphic Design
01Context

The company changed its brand name while keeping the same product, positioning, and market presence.

Under the previous name, the company had strong branded search demand, stable performance, and consistent MQL volume. The name itself carried equity and recognition in the market. When the name changed, user behavior didn’t immediately follow.

People continued searching for the old brand, while the new name had little to no established search intent. Almost overnight, branded performance dropped, not because the product changed, but because search behavior hadn’t caught up.

The challenge wasn’t about repositioning the company. It was about protecting existing demand and carefully migrating it to the new name over a 12-month transition, without losing visibility or quality.

02Problem

During Q1, immediately after the brand change, the company experienced:

  • A significant drop in branded search demand
  • Higher CPCs due to auction competition and lower Quality Scores
  • Lower MQL volume and more low-intent or spam submissions
  • Reduced visibility in Auction Insights

At the same time, I needed to:

  • Capture users still searching for the old name
  • Transition toward exclusive use of the new name
  • Prevent competitors from intercepting legacy traffic
  • Align with strict brand guidelines and rebuild branded demand under the new name
03Goals
  • Retain as much legacy branded demand as possible
  • Prevent competitor capture of high-intent searches
  • Rebuild search intent around the new brand name
  • Stabilize CPC and Quality Score
  • Restore MQL volume to pre-rebrand levels
  • Create a sustainable branded search structure for the future
04What I did

Instead of treating this as a simple brand campaign management task, I approached it as a structured demand recovery and stabilization project.

01

Protected legacy demand strategically

  • Continued bidding on old brand keywords during the transition
  • Gradually managed out old brand messaging to reduce confusion
  • Monitored Auction Insights to defend against competitor activity
02

Rebuilt campaign structure & conversion logic

  • Refined keyword match-type structure and ran controlled experiments
  • Improved ad relevance and landing page alignment
  • Added sitelinks and optimized SERP visibility
03

Leveraged first-party data & segmentation

  • Applied observation audiences to identify high-intent demand segments
  • Uploaded CRM lists to build structured retargeting layers
  • Adjusted bidding based on audience intent signals
04

Launched parallel display campaigns for the new name

  • Reinforced recognition of the new name among existing and potential users
  • Increased visibility of the new brand name
  • Aligned messaging with company guidelines during the name change phase
05Results

Performance recovery became visible from Q3 onward, after structural changes were implemented in Q2.

Q1

Significant drop in branded demand after the name change

Q2

Structural and audience adjustments implemented

Q3–Q4

Stabilization and gradual recovery

Compared to the Q1 low point:

~18%lower branded CPC
~30%more MQL volume
HigherQuality Scores across core keywords
Fewerlow-intent submissions, better auction visibility against competitors

By the end of the year, branded performance reached near pre-name-change levels under the new brand identity.

06What I learned

A brand name change may seem like a small operational shift internally, but in paid search it creates immediate performance risk. Search behavior doesn’t automatically adapt to corporate decisions, it follows familiarity.

Brand equity lives in user habits. When the name changes, those habits don’t disappear overnight. If unmanaged, that gap can quickly turn into lost demand and increased competitor pressure. Paid search is not just a performance channel during transitional moments, it’s a protective layer for existing demand.

Most importantly, stability often comes from structure, not budget. Careful migration, clean tracking, audience refinement, and controlled experimentation can rebuild performance even after a sharp decline.
